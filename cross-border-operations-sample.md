# Cross-Border Recruitment Operations: Practice Case Study

**Portfolio demonstration | Created November 2026 | Fictional scenario**

This self-directed practice sample demonstrates a simple way to coordinate a client requirement, track candidate file readiness, and send clear status updates. The client, vacancy, dates, and candidate records are fictional. No real applicant information is included. This is not client work, immigration advice, a placement result, or a claim of using a particular ATS. It was prepared with AI assistance; all example content is synthetic.

## Scenario

A fictional employer asks for three warehouse associates for a site in Poland. The employer supplies its own role requirements and document checklist. The recruiter records the agreed requirements, coordinates questions with the client, and tracks whether each file is complete enough for the next review.

**Boundary:** A recruiter can check a file against an employer-provided list and flag missing or unclear items. The recruiter does not determine visa eligibility or replace the employer, embassy, or qualified immigration adviser.

## 2. Fictional candidate file tracker

Use fictional IDs and minimum-status notes in the tracker. Keep identity documents in the employer-approved secure system, with access limited to authorized people.

| Candidate ID | Stage | Checklist status | Next action | Owner | Target date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-101 | Initial file review | CV received; one client-listed item missing | Ask candidate to provide the missing item through the approved channel | Recruiter | 24 Nov 2026 |

“Complete” here means only that the listed items were present for the next review. It does not mean the candidate is qualified, hired, or eligible for a visa.

## 3. File-check routine

1. Confirm the latest checklist version with the client before reviewing files.
2. Record only the candidate ID, checklist item, status, date checked, and next action in the tracker.
3. Mark items as **received**, **missing**, **unclear**, or **to confirm**. Avoid copying document numbers into general notes.
4. If something is missing or unclear, send a specific request through the approved channel and set a follow-up date.
5. Escalate conflicting instructions, suspected document issues, or legal and visa questions to the authorized client contact or qualified adviser. Do not edit a candidate's document.
6. Record who owns the next step, then send the client a concise progress update.

## 5. Handoff and recordkeeping checklist

- Keep the client-approved job brief and document checklist version with the role record.
- Give every next action one named owner and a due date.
- Use employer-approved storage and access controls for personal documents.
- Keep tracker notes factual, minimal, and job-related.
- Confirm interview times with the relevant time zone.
- Follow the employer's consent, retention, and deletion rules.
- Do not promise a hiring outcome, visa result, or timeline that the authorized party has not confirmed.
